Browser QAOA simulates one circuit layer and 512 measurement shots for each hour, then chooses the lowest-cost sampled schedule that passes the visible checks. It uses a simpler cost model than the Python QAOA solver and omits its slack bits, look-ahead, and local search. Results can vary between runs. It is not the run we did on an actual quantum computer. Published local experiment

How we combined QAOA with MILP

We used a quantum optimization method to propose a starting schedule, then gave that schedule to a classical solver that completes the full 24-hour optimization.

01

Propose with QAOA

A small scheduling problem groups hours into blocks. QAOA samples possible plant on/off choices. The published small-fleet experiments simulated those quantum circuits locally; the larger cases used classical simulated annealing because their problems were too large for this QAOA setup.

02

Check and repair

Classical code checks plant limits and repairs a candidate into a valid 24-hour schedule. That schedule becomes a warm start: a feasible answer given to the MILP solver before it begins searching.

03

Finish with MILP

A mixed-integer linear program (MILP) models the full day, including on/off decisions, operating rules, and cost. HiGHS improves the starting schedule and proves the best answer it finds is optimal. The warm start changes where HiGHS begins, not what the MILP is allowed to solve.

Why combine them? For now, HiGHS solving the MILP is faster and more reliable than using QAOA alone for these cases. QAOA can still be useful when it supplies a good feasible schedule before HiGHS starts: the solver has a strong answer immediately and may spend less time finding and proving the optimum. In the published comparisons below, the warm-started HiGHS solve stage was slightly faster. QAOA preparation, sampling, and repair time were not included, so these figures do not show an end-to-end speedup or a quantum advantage. They show why the hybrid approach is promising.

The charts compare cold HiGHS (no supplied schedule) with warm-started HiGHS on 24-hour cases with 5, 10, 20, and 40 plants. They are archived local results, not calculations made from your edits on this page. The browser QAOA option on the Schedule page is a separate, simplified demonstration.
